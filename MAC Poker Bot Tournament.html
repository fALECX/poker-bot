Developer setup: the macpoker SDK

Everything you need to build and test your bot locally.

1. Grab the template

Download the bot template. It contains a ready-to-run main.py and a README with every command you need.

2. Install the SDK

pip install https://poker.monashcoding.com/dl/macpoker-0.1.0-py3-none-any.whl

Python 3.10 or newer. The tournament sandbox ships Python 3.12 with the SDK and numpy preinstalled. Nothing else is available at runtime, so keep your bot to the standard library plus numpy.

3. Write your bot

from macpoker import Bot

class MyBot(Bot):
    def act(self, state):
        if state.to_call == 0:
            return state.check()
        pot_odds = state.to_call / (state.pot + state.to_call)
        if pot_odds < 0.3:
            return state.call()
        return state.fold()

Keep the entry file named main.py. You can split your code into more modules in the same folder and import them from main.py. The state gives you your hole cards, the board, the pot, stacks, legal actions and the full action history; work the numbers out yourself, that is the game.

4. Test it

macpoker play main.py house:call house:random --deals 50

Every argument after play is one seat at the table: your own files or house bots (house:call, house:checkfold, house:allin, house:random). That plays your bot against two house bots over the exact wire protocol the tournament uses. Add --subprocess for full production parity, and --history out.json to dump the hand history.

5. Submit

Zip your folder with main.py at the root and upload it from this page. A single .py also works if that is all you have.

Read the docs, ask questions

The full reference (GameState fields, actions, clocks, verdicts, the wire protocol) lives at docs.poker.monashcoding.com. Questions go in the hackathon Discord.

Rules and format

Rules

  • Bot submissions must use the provided template.
  • AI tools (Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot and so on) are allowed for development: writing code, debugging, brainstorming. They are not allowed at runtime. Your bot's in-game decisions must come from logic your team wrote, not from a live call to an AI/LLM or any other API or external service during a match. The match sandbox has no network access and submissions are audited.
  • Bots must be built entirely within the 48 hours. No code beforehand.
  • Teams of 1 to 4 members.
  • No teaming. Your bot plays for itself alone: it must not recognise, signal to, soft-play or pass chips to another team's bot, and teams must not agree on strategies that help each other.
  • Bots compute only on their own turn, inside act() and the observer hooks. No background threads, extra processes or work while opponents are acting.
  • No interfering with the sandbox, the engine or other bots, and no recovering hidden information such as unseen cards by any means other than playing the game.
  • Every final submission is read by the organisers before the tournament runs, for code quality and compliance with these rules. Breaking a rule disqualifies the team.

The full rules are in the docs.

Format

  • Teams are split into groups, about 5 bots per group depending on turnout.
  • Each round, every group plays one game per bot at the table (5 games for a group of 5). Every game deals the same 100 hands with the bots shifted one seat along, so across a round every bot plays every hand from every position, cancelling out card luck and positional advantage.
  • Bots are ranked within each game by final chip count, and each rank converts to points: most chips gets 5 points, 2nd gets 4, down to 1 for the fewest (the scale follows the group size).
  • When a group's games are done, each bot's points across them are added up. Bots are ranked within the group by that total, and the rank converts to round placement points on the same scale (1st in the group gets 5, down to 1 for last).
  • After each round, all bots are re-ranked by their cumulative round placement points and regrouped: top scorers together, the next tier together, and so on. You increasingly face bots of similar skill as the tournament goes on.
  • After 4 rounds, final placements are decided by the sum of round placement points across all rounds. The top 3 win prizes.
  • Ties are settled with a head-to-head match between the tied bots.

FAQ

What is a poker bot hackathon?

An event where participants, individually or in teams, build a bot that plays poker and uses strategy to compete against other participants' bots.

When is it?

48 hours, from Friday 2 October at 6pm to Sunday 4 October at 6pm.

Why take part?

  • A prize pool of $1000 up for grabs.
  • Team up with other participants, make new friends and build something fun together.
  • Hands-on experience in programming, game theory and strategy on a real project.
  • Push your creativity and solve interesting problems in a limited amount of time.
  • Finish with a bot and a tournament result you can showcase to future employers or put in your portfolio.
  • Watch your bot compete live at the closing ceremony.

Who is running it?

The Monash Association of Coding (MAC). Our goal is to get students industry ready and give them a collaborative setting to solve technical challenges, build projects and learn from one another.

Can we work on our bot beforehand?

No. You may only work on your bot during the 48-hour window, from 6pm Friday 2 October to 6pm Sunday 4 October. No code specific to your bot can be written before the window opens.

I have never been to a hackathon. Is that okay?

Absolutely. Most participants are beginners joining their first ever hackathon. No prior experience is required, just curiosity and a willingness to learn.

What skills do I need?

No prior poker or coding experience is required. Teams mix skill levels, and what matters most is a willingness to learn, collaborate and have fun.

Who can sign up?

Current students enrolled at any Australian university or TAFE. You do not have to be Melbourne based: the hackathon runs virtually, so you can take part from anywhere.

How do I sign up?

  1. Join the Discord.
  2. Register your team on this site, listing all of your teammates.
  3. Show up in person to the opening ceremony or tune in online.

How much does it cost?

$5 for MAC members, $10 for non-members. Become a member at monashcoding.com.

I don't have a team. Can I still sign up?

Yes. Compete as an individual, or find a team on the Discord or at the opening ceremony.

What is the team size?

1 to 4 members per team.

Can we use AI?

AI tools for coding, debugging, brainstorming and research are allowed. AI, LLM or any other network calls at runtime are not: your bot's in-game decisions must come from code your team wrote.

How are the winners decided?

Bots are grouped and play a series of games each round, earning points based on their final chip count in each game. Results across all 4 rounds decide the rankings, and the top three win prizes. The games are replayed in person at the closing ceremony and on this site to watch from home.

What do we build the bot in?

Python, using our provided bot template and game engine. Both unlock when the hackathon starts.
